#+title: V11 — Galvanostatic dynamic PEMFC model
#+description: Physics-first architecture for current-driven dynamic PEMFC simulation

* Objective

V11 targets a dynamic PEMFC model driven by experimentally meaningful inputs:

- stack current I(t);
- cathode air flow Q_air(t);
- anode purge command u_purge(t);
- ambient/inlet thermodynamic conditions when required.

Primary predicted outputs are:

- stack voltage V_stack(t);
- stack temperature T_stack(t);
- membrane hydration state;
- anode and cathode gas inventories;
- physically derived estimates of liquid-water inventory on both sides.

The electrical formulation is galvanostatic: current is imposed and voltage is
predicted.

* Scientific constraint

V11 SHALL NOT introduce free empirical tuning coefficients whose purpose is to
make simulated trajectories match a target data set.

Every model parameter must belong to one of the following provenance classes:

1. fundamental physical constant;
2. measured or manufacturer-specified stack/material/geometry parameter;
3. independently measured material property;
4. published mechanistic constitutive law or transport correlation, with its
   source and validity range recorded;
5. quantity derived algebraically from items 1--4.

Published correlations may contain fitted constants from their source
literature, but these constants must not be re-fitted to the Ballard target
data merely to improve agreement.

Any parameter whose provenance cannot be established must be marked
UNRESOLVED rather than silently calibrated.

* Modelling hierarchy

The existing Dedalus spatial models remain the high-fidelity reference.

V11 is not intended to discard their physics. It is intended to construct a
dynamic model whose reduced states and closures are derived from the same
physical balances and transport laws.

The hierarchy is:

  high-fidelity spatial model
  -> physically justified reduction / averaging
  -> dynamic galvanostatic system model.

Reduction is allowed; phenomenological curve fitting is not.

* Inputs

** Electrical

  I_stack(t)

The imposed stack current determines the electrochemical reaction rate through
Faraday's law.

** Cathode

  Q_air(t)

Air flow controls oxygen supply, convective heat removal, water-vapour removal
and therefore concentration, temperature and hydration dynamics.

** Anode

  u_purge(t)

The purge command changes anode mass inventories through an explicit outlet
mass-flow balance. Purge volume, valve timing and anode geometry must remain
physical parameters rather than fitted gains.

* Candidate dynamic states

The initial state set is intentionally physical rather than minimal:

  x = [
    T_stack,
    lambda_mem,
    n_H2_a,
    n_N2_a,
    n_H2O_v_a,
    n_O2_c,
    n_N2_c,
    n_H2O_v_c,
    m_H2O_l_a,
    m_H2O_l_c
  ]^T

States may later be removed only if a derivation shows that a quasi-steady
approximation is justified on the relevant time scale.

* Governing physics

** Electrochemistry

Current is prescribed.

Local or effective overpotential is obtained from the inverse electrochemical
kinetic relation rather than by solving an unconstrained current source.

The electrochemical source terms must satisfy Faraday stoichiometry exactly.

** Gas inventories

Species dynamics are written as molar conservation equations:

  accumulation = inlet - outlet - electrochemical consumption/production
                 + interphase transfer.

No first-order lag may replace a species balance unless derived from the
balance and geometry.

** Membrane water

Membrane hydration shall include physically derived water transport mechanisms,
at minimum where relevant:

- electro-osmotic drag;
- back diffusion;
- water exchange with adjacent gas/catalyst layers.

Transport coefficients must come from explicit material laws or literature
correlations, with temperature and hydration dependence retained where known.

** Thermal balance

Temperature follows an energy balance:

  thermal storage
  = electrochemical/irreversible heat generation
    - convective heat removal
    - other explicitly modelled heat losses.

No fitted thermal time constant is permitted if heat capacity, geometry and
heat-transfer mechanisms can be represented directly.

** Liquid water

Liquid-water states are mass inventories, not arbitrary saturation filters.

Production, condensation, evaporation, capillary transport, drainage and purge
removal shall be represented only through explicit mass balances and
physically documented constitutive relations.

If a reliable mechanistic closure is unavailable, the corresponding mechanism
must remain identified as unresolved rather than replaced with a fitted gain.

* Voltage prediction

The model output is

  V_stack(t) = N_cells * V_cell(t).

V_cell must be obtained from reversible potential and physically represented
losses, including as applicable:

- activation;
- membrane/protonic ohmic loss;
- electronic ohmic loss;
- concentration / reactant-transport loss.

The imposed current is therefore an input constraint, while voltage is a
predicted observable.

* Validation philosophy

Validation and calibration are distinct.

Validation compares predictions against independent observations.

Calibration may only identify genuinely physical quantities that are
independently uncertain but measurable in principle, and such identification
must be explicitly documented.

Calibration SHALL NOT introduce compensating coefficients to hide missing
physics.

Each V11 validation result should therefore report:

- parameter provenance;
- conservation residuals;
- numerical tolerances;
- predicted voltage and temperature;
- water inventories;
- sensitivity to spatial/time discretization;
- unresolved physics.

* Initial implementation trajectory

1. Freeze V10 as the spatial/high-fidelity reference.
2. Implement the galvanostatic electrochemical algebra with current imposed.
3. Add dynamic cathode species balances driven by Q_air(t).
4. Add dynamic stack thermal balance.
5. Add dead-end anode H2/N2/H2O balances and explicit purge events.
6. Add membrane water transport from electro-osmotic drag and back diffusion.
7. Add physically based phase-change/liquid-water inventories.
8. Validate conservation and limiting cases before comparison with stack data.
